For a 10-mark question, structure your answer clearly. Start with a definition, break down each of the four properties (Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability), and explicitly tie them to the banking example provided.

ACID properties are fundamental to ensuring data integrity in a database management system, especially during concurrent processing or system failures.

1. Atomicity (The "All or Nothing" Rule)

Ensures that a transaction is treated as a single, indivisible logical unit of work. Either all operations execute successfully, or none do.

Banking Example: If Rs. 500 is deducted from Account A, it must be credited to Account B. If the system crashes after deduction but before crediting, atomicity ensures the deduction is rolled back.
2. Consistency

Ensures the database transitions from one valid state to another, maintaining all predefined rules (constraints, cascades, triggers).

Banking Example: The total sum of money in Account A and Account B before the transaction must equal the total sum after the transaction.
